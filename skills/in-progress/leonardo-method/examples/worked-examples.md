# Worked examples — the method in action

Three full passes through the four phases.

## Studying a chapter of an analysis textbook

- **Phase 1:** Read Chapter 4 on sequences and series. Mark passages, write definitions in the margins, note questions in red.
- **Phase 2:** Walk for an hour. Three insights about how Cauchy sequences relate to completeness surface. Capture them in a pocket notebook.
- **Phase 3:** At the whiteboard, write the entire theory from memory. Discover three gaps. Look them up. Erase. Repeat twice more. Solve fifteen problems from the book.
- **Phase 4:** Write a six-page note, "Sequences and Series — own reconstruction," with proofs in your own notation, four worked examples, and links to the chapters on continuity and integration.

## Internalizing a technical paper on a new ML technique

- **Phase 1:** Read the paper twice — fast, then annotated. Mark the confusing sections.
- **Phase 2:** Discuss with colleagues over coffee; watch the authors' talk; capture the insights.
- **Phase 3:** Implement the technique from scratch, without copying the reference implementation. Run it on a toy problem. Compare results.
- **Phase 4:** Write a blog post or team note explaining the technique, its strengths and weaknesses, and its connections to methods you already know.

## Mastering a new programming language

- **Phase 1:** Read the official "Book"/introduction. Annotate. Try snippets in a REPL.
- **Phase 2:** Build small toy programs. Note the design patterns that confuse. Discuss with the community.
- **Phase 3:** Reimplement a small project from another language in this one, without language-specific tutorials. Find the idiom gaps, look them up, repeat with a second project.
- **Phase 4:** Write a personal cheat sheet and a "how I think in this language now" reflection, linked to related languages.
